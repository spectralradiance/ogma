## First Light

Before the sky is dark it turns a colour that has no good name — deeper than blue, not yet black, the colour of ink held up to a lamp. That is when the first star appears, and more often than not it is not a star at all. It is Venus, bright and steady, a planet borrowing the Sun's light while the Sun itself has gone.

Stand outside long enough and the stars seem to arrive one by one. They do not arrive, of course; they were there all along. What changes is the eye. It takes the better part of half an hour for our night vision to wake, for the rods at the edge of sight to take over from the cones that serve us in daylight.

Night, then, is not something that falls. It is something we grow into, a little at a time, if we are patient enough to wait for it.
